# Co-Intelligence

(2024)
by Ethan Mollick

- Starts with a relatable story: a professor describes that same mix of excitement and anxiety many of us felt trying an LLM for the first time—especially the immediate worry about what it means for our careers.

- We're already seeing the ripple effects in classrooms, where students are openly questioning if the professions they're studying for will even exist in a few years.

- Unlike the internet, which took decades to fully take over, LLMs achieved massive consumer adoption almost overnight, giving us very little time to adapt.

- Governments are scrambling to regulate training data, raising tricky questions around copyright, data ownership, and how to stop models from amplifying existing societal biases.

- The bigger ethical headache is that LLMs are black boxes that can sound totally confident while being completely wrong, making accountability a massive gray area when AI decisions go sideways.
